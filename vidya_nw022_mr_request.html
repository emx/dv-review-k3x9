Paragraph 2
English
The print is a sārtha: verse carried inside commentary. Here the commentary of Shri Shridhar Swami is set aside by selection and only the mūla — the ovī text itself — is kept. Nothing else is altered. The working rule of this edition admits no exception: the book is never changed to fit the instrument.

Paragraph 3
English
The text was not retyped. The print's own digital layer, damaged by the encoding it was born with, was recovered whole — each broken glyph traced back to the character it stood for through a fixed table: deterministic, repeatable, checkable.

Paragraph 4
English
Recovery invites doubt, so the recovered text was made to earn trust. It was read against independent witnesses of the Dāsbodh — the Wikisource lineage and other digital editions — and where the witnesses parted ways, the disputed sites were put to a bilingual reader against the printed page itself, in measured sessions with planted controls, so that even the checking was checked.

Paragraph 5
English
Every verse printed here is tied, byte for byte, to a pinned master text recorded in the colophon; presentation is never allowed to rewrite the pāṭha. Where sources agree, the reading stands silently; every remaining doubt is kept visible in the apparatus.

Paragraph 6
English
This edition does not claim perfection. It claims a traceable reading: what was printed, why, and what remains open can each be checked — the full accounting stands in the colophon at the end of this volume.
